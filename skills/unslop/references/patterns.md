# Unslop pattern catalog

## Remove

- Empty openings such as "Certainly" or "Great question".
- Closing offers that add no information.
- Sycophantic agreement.
- "It is important to note that" and similar throat-clearing.
- Generic conclusions that state no fact or next action.
- Decorative emoji and excessive bold labels.
- Em dashes in editable prose. Rewrite the sentence with periods or commas.
  Preserve protected verbatim content unchanged.

## Usually rewrite

- Puffery such as "pivotal", "groundbreaking", or "testament to".
- Promotional adjectives that do not describe a measurable property.
- Vague attribution such as "experts believe".
- Inflated verbs such as "utilize", "leverage", and "facilitate".
- Abstract phrases such as "serves as", "evolving landscape", or "interplay".
- "Not just X, but Y" constructions.
- Forced groups of three.
- False "from X to Y" ranges whose endpoints are not a meaningful scale.
- Repeated synonyms for one concept.
- Passive voice when the actor matters.
- Adverbs standing in for a mechanism or measurement.
- Sentences that could appear unchanged in an unrelated project's
  documentation.

## Review in context

These patterns can be legitimate. Change them only when they reduce clarity:

- specialized technical vocabulary;
- passive voice when the actor is unknown or irrelevant;
- long sentences expressing one coherent relationship;
- colons before examples or lists;
- parentheses containing useful secondary information;
- natural groups of three;
- evidence-calibrated hedging.

## Add human voice

- React to evidence instead of mechanically listing advantages and
  disadvantages.
- Use concrete examples and consequences.
- Let short sentences land important points.
- Use longer sentences when a condition and consequence belong together.
- Express a reasoned view in explanation and analysis.
- Keep reference material dry and factual.

## Final questions

1. Does any sentence make a large claim without specific support?
2. Does the prose repeat a predictable template?
3. Did the rewrite erase a caveat or lower the apparent uncertainty?
4. Did plain wording reduce technical precision?
5. Does the result sound appropriate for this author and audience?
